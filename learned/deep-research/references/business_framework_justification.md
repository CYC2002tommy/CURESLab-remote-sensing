# Theoretical Justification for Business & Policy Frameworks

When generating, researching, or proposing a structured business framework (such as decarbonization frameworks, MCDA models, or compliance matrices) for corporate or SME clients, **always proactively include a section on theoretical and strategic justification.**

## Why this is necessary
Clients and academic stakeholders need to understand *why* they should adopt a structured framework rather than addressing problems ad-hoc (e.g., just replacing LED lights instead of an integrated strategy). 

## Key Arguments to Source in Literature
When conducting a `deep-research` pass for framework design, explicitly search for and include literature supporting these angles:
1. **Preventing Siloed Decisions & Sub-optimal Investments:** Ad-hoc approaches lead to resource misallocation. Frameworks force holistic evaluation (e.g., Scope 1/2/3 impacts).
2. **Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDA):** Frameworks act as decision support systems (DSS), reducing "decision paralysis" for SMEs with limited CAPEX.
3. **Strategic Risk Translation:** Frameworks convert ambiguous regulatory risks (like EU CBAM or carbon pricing) into actionable operational blueprints.

*Implementation Note:* Integrate these arguments into the final `.docx` or whitepaper report to elevate it from a mere "list of actions" to a "strategic imperative."
